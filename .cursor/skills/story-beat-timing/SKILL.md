# Story Beat Timing for Engaging Video Blocks

Use this skill to make Scatter videos feel engaging as a sequence, not just a collection of animated blocks.

This skill focuses on:
- Pacing
- Rhythm
- Scene structure
- VO sync
- Music sync
- Transition logic
- Emotional payoff
- Block-to-block flow

## Core Rule

A good video has beats.

A beat is a moment where the viewer receives new information, feels a shift, or sees a payoff.

Motion should serve the beat.

A Scatter video should not feel like:
- Template 1
- Template 2
- Template 3
- End card

It should feel like:
- Setup
- Build
- Reveal
- Proof
- Payoff
- CTA

## Common Problem

Many motion tools create videos where:
- Every scene has the same pacing.
- Every transition feels similar.
- Everything animates constantly.
- There is no contrast between calm and impact.
- There is no setup or payoff.
- The edit ignores VO and music.
- Blocks look okay individually but boring together.
- The video feels like a slideshow of animated layouts.

Scatter should avoid this by giving each block a role in the video.

## Block Roles

Every block should have one primary role.

### 1. Hook

Purpose:
Grab attention quickly.

Motion style:
- Fast visual entry
- Strong contrast
- Bold type
- Immediate focal point
- Sharp timing
- Minimal explanation
- High visual confidence

Good for:
- First 1–3 seconds
- Social ads
- Product launches
- Big claims

Transitions:
- Hard cut
- Smash cut
- Fast mask reveal
- Snap zoom
- Beat-synced type hit

### 2. Problem

Purpose:
Show tension, pain, complexity, or before-state.

Motion style:
- Slight friction
- Slower movement
- Compression
- Clutter to clarity
- Blocked motion
- Shake/glitch only when brand-appropriate

Good for:
- Before state
- User frustration
- Market problem
- Complexity

Transitions:
- Compression into reveal
- Glitch into solution
- Clutter wipes away
- Mask closes before product reveal

### 3. Reveal

Purpose:
Introduce the solution or main product moment.

Motion style:
- Anticipation
- Mask reveal
- Camera push
- Clean stage
- Satisfying landing
- Strong hold

Good for:
- Product reveal
- Feature intro
- Logo/product moment

Transitions:
- Camera push-through
- Shape opens
- Product card enters from previous motion
- Problem elements clear away

### 4. Proof

Purpose:
Show evidence.

Motion style:
- Data animation
- Counters
- Graphs
- Cards
- Comparisons
- Sequential reveals
- Clear visual order

Good for:
- Stats
- Benefits
- Testimonials
- Feature list

Transitions:
- Data point becomes next object
- Stat card wipes to feature card
- Graph line draws into next scene
- Match cut between cards

### 5. Detail

Purpose:
Explain a feature.

Motion style:
- Slower and clearer
- Highlight key UI
- Cursor/focus movement
- Step-by-step sequencing
- Less decorative motion

Good for:
- Product walkthrough
- UI explanation
- Feature education

Transitions:
- UI area zooms into next feature
- Cursor guides camera
- Highlight shape becomes next frame
- Match cut from one UI panel to another

### 6. Brand Beat

Purpose:
Create emotion, vibe, and memorability.

Motion style:
- More expressive
- More abstract
- More visual
- Music-led
- Texture/shape/camera-driven
- Less explanatory

Good for:
- Brand personality
- Mid-video energy shift
- Visual reset
- Lifestyle or mood moment

Transitions:
- Texture wipe
- Light leak
- Smash cut
- Abstract shape morph
- Camera whip
- Film/grain transition

### 7. CTA

Purpose:
Make the viewer act.

Motion style:
- Clear landing
- Strong hold
- CTA button/card emphasis
- Logo resolution
- Less competing motion
- Clean composition

Good for:
- End card
- Download prompt
- Sign up
- Visit site
- Product waitlist

Transitions:
- Proof resolves into CTA
- Product UI collapses into logo/card
- Brand shape frames CTA
- Final beat logo resolve

## Beat Structure

A simple 15-second product video:

1. Hook — 0:00–0:03
2. Problem/Context — 0:03–0:05
3. Product Reveal — 0:05–0:08
4. Proof/Feature — 0:08–0:12
5. CTA/Logo — 0:12–0:15

A simple 30-second product video:

1. Hook — 0:00–0:04
2. Problem — 0:04–0:08
3. Reveal — 0:08–0:12
4. Feature 1 — 0:12–0:17
5. Feature 2 — 0:17–0:22
6. Proof/Benefit — 0:22–0:26
7. CTA — 0:26–0:30

A social ad can be:

1. Visual hook
2. One sentence promise
3. Product proof
4. Fast feature demo
5. CTA

A product launch video can be:

1. Brand mood
2. Problem tension
3. Product reveal
4. Feature rhythm
5. Proof
6. Logo/CTA

## Timing Contrast

Engaging videos need contrast.

Use:
- Fast → slow
- Dense → simple
- Quiet → impact
- Detail → brand beat
- Motion → hold
- Masked → open
- Small → large
- Flat → depth
- UI detail → emotional brand moment
- Abstract → product clarity

Avoid:
- Same speed for every block.
- Every block using the same transition.
- Constant motion with no rest.
- Long explanations with no visual payoff.
- Visual effects on every scene.
- Every transition being a fade/slide.

## VO Sync

When VO exists, motion should follow speech structure.

Sync to:
- Sentence start
- Key noun
- Key verb
- Benefit phrase
- Contrast words like "but", "now", "without", "finally"
- CTA phrase
- Final brand line

Rules:
- Do not animate important text before VO says it unless used as a hook.
- Do not cut away during key words.
- Add hold time after dense phrases.
- Land visual proof on the claim.
- Use motion accents on emphasized words.
- Use transitions during pauses, breath marks, or sentence changes.
- Avoid major transitions in the middle of a phrase.

Example:
VO: "Launch polished product videos in minutes."

Timing:
- "Launch" = scene starts
- "polished product videos" = product/video preview reveals
- "in minutes" = stat/badge pops or timer snaps in

## Music Sync

When music exists, use it to create structure.

Sync:
- Scene cuts to downbeats
- Big reveals to drops
- Text accents to snares/claps
- Small details to hi-hats
- CTA/logo to final beat
- Transitions to bars/phrases, not every beat

Avoid:
- Cutting against the beat accidentally.
- Every single beat triggering motion.
- Audio-driven chaos.
- Music loop with no edit structure.
- Constant micro-animation that fights the track.

## Transition Logic

Transitions should carry meaning.

Types:
- Match cut: similar shape or layout continues between scenes.
- Wipe: one visual pushes another away.
- Mask reveal: new idea appears inside old shape.
- Camera move: viewer travels through the layout.
- Smash cut: sudden energy shift.
- Dissolve/fade: soft emotional change.
- Glitch: disruption or tech/error moment.
- Morph: transformation or evolution.
- Texture wipe: brand/material-based scene change.
- Object carry-through: one object becomes the next scene's anchor.

Use transitions intentionally:
- Problem → Reveal: friction clears into solution.
- Feature → Feature: match-cut between UI areas.
- Proof → CTA: data resolves into action.
- Brand Beat → Product: abstract shape becomes interface.
- Hook → Detail: bold claim snaps into product evidence.
- Detail → Proof: UI interaction becomes stat/result.

Avoid:
- Random transition library feeling.
- Same transition every time.
- Glitch transitions for calm/premium brands.
- Transitions that hide the message.
- Transitions that ignore the previous and next block roles.

## Shot Rhythm

Each shot should include:

1. Setup
2. Main action
3. Settle
4. Hold or exit

Even fast shots need a tiny settle.

Example:
- Setup: background or camera prepares
- Main action: headline/product reveal
- Settle: overshoot/follow-through ends
- Hold: viewer reads
- Exit: next block begins

For transitions:
- Exit anticipation: outgoing scene prepares
- Bridge action: transition carries viewer
- Entry impact: incoming scene lands
- Settle: new scene becomes readable

## Block Sequence Rules

When arranging blocks:

- Do not use two high-energy blocks back to back unless the goal is intensity.
- Do not use two dense UI explanation blocks back to back without a reset.
- Add a brand beat after dense information.
- Add a hold after a big reveal.
- Make the CTA simpler than the previous block.
- Use transition variety, but keep a consistent motion language.
- Repeat one motion motif across the video for brand cohesion.
- Build energy toward the CTA or logo.
- Avoid ending with weak motion or a static fade.

## Last 10% Questions

Ask these after the video already works:

1. Where does the viewer's eye go first?
2. Where is the most satisfying moment?
3. Is there a moment of surprise?
4. Is there enough breathing room?
5. Does the edit build energy?
6. Does the motion have a clear rhythm?
7. Does each transition have a reason?
8. Are we overusing fades/slides?
9. Does the video feel like a template?
10. Would this still feel good muted?
11. Would this feel better with audio?
12. Does the last frame sell the brand?
13. Does every block know what came before and after?
14. Does the sequence have contrast?
15. Does the CTA feel earned?

## Engagement Checklist

Before a video is approved, check:

- Does the first second have a strong hook?
- Does every block have a role?
- Is there pacing contrast?
- Are important moments held long enough?
- Are transitions varied but coherent?
- Does motion support the VO?
- Does motion acknowledge music?
- Is there at least one memorable visual moment?
- Does the ending feel resolved?
- Does the final CTA have enough clarity?
- Does the video feel branded, not generic?
- Does the sequence avoid slideshow energy?

## Output Format When Using This Skill

When improving a sequence, respond with:

- Current pacing diagnosis
- Beat map
- Block roles
- Shot-by-shot timing changes
- Transition recommendations between every block
- VO sync recommendations
- Music sync recommendations
- Energy curve
- Final CTA/end-card improvements
